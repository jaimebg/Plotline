Thank you for the detailed feedback across our previous submissions. Re-reading it against the app as it shipped, we think the reviewer's impression was fair, and we have addressed the cause rather than restated our case.

**What we found when we looked.** Several of the things we would have pointed to were unreachable. The Stats tab was hidden behind a check for saved user data, so on a fresh install — which is what a reviewer sees — it showed an empty state and nothing else, while the comparison tools, career profiles and trend explorers behind it needed no user data at all. An episode-quality chart had been disconnected from the detail screen by an earlier change and was never rendered. And the app was built for iPhone only, so on the iPad Air used for the review it ran in compatibility mode, in a phone-sized window on a large display.

**What version 1.4.0 changes.**

- **Our own analysis is now on every series screen.** Plotline computes, from every episode rating of a series, where its quality declines and never recovers, how consistent it is, whether it starts slow or hooks early, whether it lands its ending, which episodes stand far above or below their own season, and a 0-100 Plotline Score shown together with the three components it is made of. Each verdict is displayed with the figures that produced it. None of this is available from TMDB or any other source — it is derived in the app.

- **The app has content on first launch, offline.** 122 fully analysed series ship inside the binary, grouped into lists we derived ourselves — "Shows That Never Slip", "Knows When It Peaked" — that no catalogue provides. All five tabs show real content with no account and no network.

- **It is now a native iPad app**, with layouts that use the screen rather than a scaled-up phone window.

- **Where to watch**, per title and per region, with a region selector so availability is not limited to our own country.

We have put step-by-step directions in the App Review Notes so none of this depends on the reviewer happening to scroll to the right place — that is the mistake we made in the previous submissions.

We would appreciate a call through the Resolution Center. If the analysis still does not meet the bar for original content, we would rather understand that directly than submit again on a guess.
